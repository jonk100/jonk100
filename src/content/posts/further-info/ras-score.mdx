# Decoding the RAS: The "Moneyball" of NFL Athleticism

In the high-stakes world of the NFL Draft, where millions of dollars are gambled on the potential of 21-year-olds, teams are constantly searching for a "cheat code" to predict success. Enter the **Relative Athletic Score (RAS)**—the metric that has transformed how fans and front offices view the NFL Combine.

If you’ve spent any time on "Draft Twitter" or followed the 2026 Combine cycle, you’ve seen the colorful green-and-red scorecards. But what does the number actually mean, and does a high score actually translate to Sunday wins?

---

### **What is RAS? The Origins**
Created by **Kent Lee Platte** (@MathBomb), RAS is a composite metric that scores NFL prospects on a scale of **0 to 10** based on their athletic testing. 

The genius of RAS isn't just the number—it's the **context**. Before RAS, a 4.55-second 40-yard dash was just a "decent" time. RAS asks: *Is that 4.55 coming from a 180-lb wide receiver or a 260-lb linebacker?*

* **Size-Adjustment:** It compares a player's metrics (height, weight, speed, explosion, and agility) against every other player at that **specific position** since 1987.
* **The Scale:** A **10.0** represents the 1-of-1 best athlete ever tested at that position. A **5.0** is perfectly average.

---

### **How to Read the Scorecard**
When looking at an RAS card, the data is typically broken into four "buckets":
1.  **Size:** Height and Weight.
2.  **Speed:** 40-yard dash, 20-yard split, and 10-yard split.
3.  **Explosion:** Vertical Jump and Broad Jump.
4.  **Agility:** 20-yard Shuttle and 3-Cone Drill.

**The Golden Rule:** Look for the "Green." Players with a score of **8.0 or higher** are considered elite athletes. In the modern NFL, over **70% of players drafted in the Top 100** typically fall into this elite athletic tier.

---

### **Does it Actually Work? The Predictive Success Rate**
The data suggests that while athleticism isn't everything, it provides a significantly higher "floor" for NFL prospects. 

* **The Roster Reality:** Historical data shows that roughly **81% of players on active NFL rosters** had an RAS of 5.0 or higher during their draft cycle.
* **The Elite Edge:** Nearly **45% of NFL starters** boast an RAS above 8.0. 
* **The "Agility" Correlation:** Studies, including those from the Harvard Sports Analysis Collective, have found that **agility metrics** (3-cone and shuttle) are often more predictive of success for WRs and Edge Rushers than the 40-yard dash alone.

---

### **Proof of Concept: High RAS Stars**
When elite tape meets an elite RAS, you usually get a superstar. These players tested as "freaks" and lived up to the billing:
* **Myles Garrett (9.99 RAS):** The definition of a "1-of-1" athlete. His combination of 270-lb size and 4.6 speed made him a perennial All-Pro.
* **Saquon Barkley (9.94 RAS):** Weighing 233 lbs and running a 4.41 while jumping 41 inches essentially broke the RAS scale for running backs.
* **Luke Kuechly (9.91 RAS):** Proof that "instinctual" linebackers can also be elite athletes. His agility scores were among the best ever for the position.

---

### **The "Outliers": Overcoming a Low RAS**
Of course, the "Under-5.0" club proves that athleticism is just one piece of the puzzle. These legends lacked the "measurable" juice but had the "football" juice:
* **Tom Brady (1.49 RAS):** The ultimate outlier. His 5.28-second 40-yard dash is the stuff of legend, yet he is the greatest to ever play the game.
* **Cooper Kupp (5.10 RAS):** A "barely average" athlete by RAS standards who became a Triple Crown receiver through elite route-running and football IQ.
* **Nick Bolton (4.60 RAS):** Despite a "below average" score, Bolton became one of the league's most productive linebackers, proving that diagnostic speed can trump raw track speed.

---

### **The Bottom Line**
RAS is a **filter, not a crystal ball**. It tells you if a player has the physical tools to survive in a league of giants. As we look toward the 2026 Draft, remember: a 10.0 RAS tells you what a player *can* do, but the film tells you what they *will* do. 

Drafting a 9.9 RAS athlete is like buying a Ferrari—it’s the fastest car on the block, but you still need a driver who knows how to handle the corners.
