# The 2026 NFL Draft: Offensive Tackle Preview (Top 10)

The 2026 offensive tackle class has officially arrived at its "put up or shut up" moment. After weeks of combine drama — short arms, DNP whispers, and a 352-pound man somehow running a respectable 40 — the gap between the "tape purists" and the "measurables merchants" has never been wider at this position. While the league's consensus leans toward Miami's Francis Mauigoa as the class anchor, scouts and executives can't agree on whether he's better suited at tackle or guard. Meanwhile, Georgia's Monroe Freeling has rocketed up boards with a near-perfect athletic profile, and Utah's Spencer Fano is either the most underrated tackle in the class or a future Pro Bowl guard, depending on who you ask.

**Daniel Jeremiah** says there are [seven OTs with first-round potential](https://www.steelers.com/news/on-the-clock-ranking-the-top-ots-in-the-26-draft), but what's universally agreed upon is that after that group, the shelf drops off dramatically. If your team needs a tackle, you might have to have to reach for one.

Below is our 2026 OT Big Board, cross-referencing the industry's top analysts with the verified athletic numbers from the combine and Pro Day circuit.

---

## Detailed Prospect Breakdowns ('26 Top 10 Offensive Tackles)

1. **Francis Mauigoa (Miami)** - The class's most polarizing figure. [**Field Yates**](https://www.espn.com/nfl/draft2026/story/_/id/47590505/2026-nfl-draft-rankings-board-top-prospects-best-positional-field-yates) and [**Mel Kiper**](https://www.espn.com/nfl/draft2026/story/_/id/46573669/2026-nfl-draft-rankings-mel-kiper-big-board-top-prospects-players-positions) both have him at OT1, and [**Bucky Brooks**](https://www.nfl.com/news/bucky-brooks-top-five-2026-nfl-draft-prospects-by-position-2-0-garrett-nussmeier-rises-up-qb-rankings) calls him a "Day 1 starter at right tackle." His 2,700+ career snaps and a dominance rate that included only two sacks allowed over two seasons is elite. The knock: his 329-lb frame and shorter-than-ideal arm length have teams debating guard vs. tackle — he bypassed combine testing entirely, so we won't have official numbers until his Miami Pro Day on March 23. The intrigue is real.

2. **Monroe Freeling (Georgia)** - The draft's biggest post-combine riser. His **4.93s 40**, **34¾" arms**, **10⅛" hands**, and 84.5" wingspan produced an unofficial **9.99 RAS** — ranking 2nd among all OTs since 1987. [**Daniel Jeremiah**](https://www.nfl.com/news/daniel-jeremiah-s-top-50-2026-nfl-draft-prospect-rankings-4-0) says he has "the most upside of any tackle in this year's draft," and [**Matt Miller**](https://www.espn.com/nfl/draft2026/story/_/id/47190881/2026-nfl-draft-rankings-matt-miller-top-prospects-players-positions) calls him a top-15 prospect. The caveat: only 18 college starts. He's the highest ceiling in the class, but also the rawest.

3. **Spencer Fano (Utah)** - The Outland Trophy winner entered Indy as many analysts' OT1, then watched his arm length (32⅛") become the combine's biggest OT storyline — well below the unofficial ~33" threshold most teams want at outside tackle, and paired with just 9" hands. But his **4.91s 40** — tied for the fastest among all OTs — and elite positional drills [earned an 'A' grade from CBS Sports](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/draft/news/2026-nfl-draft-grades-top-25-prospects-nfl-combine/). [**Bucky Brooks**](https://www.nfl.com/news/bucky-brooks-top-five-2026-nfl-draft-prospects-by-position-2-0-garrett-nussmeier-rises-up-qb-rankings) calls him "a barroom brawler" with the athleticism to thrive in any scheme. At his Utah Pro Day, a remeasurement came back at **32⅞"** — [likely enough to keep him on the outside](https://www.prideofdetroit.com/detroit-lions-draft/160282/2026-lions-mock-draft-roundup-8-0-free-agency-heavily-alters-projections).

4. **Kadyn Proctor (Alabama)** - [**Jeremiah**](https://www.steelers.com/news/on-the-clock-ranking-the-top-ots-in-the-26-draft) says Proctor has "the most upside of any blocker in this draft." The numbers back it up: at **6'7" and 352 lbs** with **34⅜" arms** and **10⅛" hands**, he ran a **5.21s 40** and posted a **32.5" vertical** — and cranked out **26 bench reps** — an absurd athletic profile for his size. He started all 39 games of his Alabama career at left tackle. The debate is whether he's the next dominant franchise left tackle or a future guard who was simply too big to run from day one.

5. **Caleb Lomu (Utah)** - Fano's Utah bookend earned a consensus first-round grade with a clean combine: **34⅛" arms**, **10¼" hands**, a **4.99s 40**, and a **32.5" vertical** produced an unofficial **9.88 RAS**. [**Bucky Brooks**](https://www.nfl.com/news/bucky-brooks-top-five-2026-nfl-draft-prospects-by-position-2-0-garrett-nussmeier-rises-up-qb-rankings) calls him "a natural left tackle with the athleticism and movement skills to shadowbox elite pass rushers." The knock is that he was ["never dominant" on tape in 2025](https://walterfootball.com/OTRankings2026.php), even as scouts "really liked" him.

6. **Blake Miller (Clemson)** - The "experience play" of the class. With **54 career starts** at right tackle — more than anyone in this group — Miller brings a technical foundation that teams coveting a plug-and-play starter covet. His **34" arms** clear the threshold comfortably, and [**Matt Miller**](https://www.espn.com/nfl/draft2026/story/_/id/47190881/2026-nfl-draft-rankings-matt-miller-top-prospects-players-positions) points to just nine pressures allowed in 2025 as a marker of a high-floor starting tackle. [**Field Yates**](https://www.espn.com/nfl/draft2026/story/_/id/47590505/2026-nfl-draft-rankings-board-top-prospects-best-positional-field-yates) has him at OT5.

7. **Max Iheanachor (Arizona State)** - The class's great "raw" mystery. A former soccer player from Nigeria who didn't play football until junior college, Iheanachor has only 32 college starts — but his **34⅛" arms** and **10" hands** give him the physical profile of a franchise tackle, and [**Jeremiah**](https://www.steelers.com/news/on-the-clock-ranking-the-top-ots-in-the-26-draft) calls his tape "full of clues hinting he could end up being one of the best offensive linemen in this year's draft class." He ran a **4.90s 40** at his Pro Day and was a Senior Bowl standout. [**Kiper**](https://www.espn.com/nfl/draft2026/story/_/id/46149416/2026-nfl-draft-rankings-top-prospects-position) has him at OT5.

8. **Jude Bowry (Boston College)** - The combine's quiet standout among tackles. His **5.08s 40** and **34.5" vertical** surprised evaluators who expected a plodder, and [CBS Sports gave him top-75 potential](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/draft/news/2026-nfl-combine-offensive-line-grades-spencer-fano-kadyn-proctor/) on the strength of his on-field control and calculated movement. The concern is his **31⅝" arms** and **9¼" hands** — both below the thresholds teams want at outside tackle — which may push interior conversion conversations regardless of how well he moves. [**PFF**](https://www.pff.com/news/draft-2026-nfl-draft-offensive-tackle-rankings) has praised his anchoring technique and zone-scheme fit, but the measurements won't go away.

9. **Caleb Tiernan (Northwestern)** - The "savvy veteran" of the class at **6'7"**. [**Matt Miller**](https://www.espn.com/nfl/draft2026/story/_/id/48299038/2026-nfl-mock-draft-seven-rounds-257-picks-projections-matt-miller) mocks him to the 49ers as a long-term successor to Trent Williams. He moves well for his enormous stature and brings the kind of football intelligence that makes offensive line coaches sleep at night. His **32⅛" arms** are a concern — identical to Fano's controversial Indy measurement — and combined with a modest **9½" hands**, he lacks both the explosive testing profile and the length of the top-five group.

10. **Austin Barber (Florida)** - A smooth, technically polished zone-blocker who quietly checks most of the schematic boxes NFL teams are looking for. His **33⅛" arms** clear the threshold, and [**PFF**](https://www.pff.com/news/draft-2026-nfl-draft-offensive-tackle-rankings) notes his low, wide pre-snap stance as a textbook leverage advantage, praising his hand placement and leg drive in the run game. He'll likely be drafted as a high-floor developmental starter — the kind of pick that quietly turns into a 10-year career.

## The Final Word: Guard Converts vs. Franchise Tackles
The elephant in the room for the 2026 OT class isn't talent — it's positional certainty. Three of the top five prospects (**Mauigoa**, **Fano**, and potentially **Proctor**) have legitimate guard conversion questions hanging over them heading into draft weekend. That's not normal. It's also what makes this class so fascinating.

For teams at the top of the board, the choice is stark: do you take the most NFL-ready mauler in **Mauigoa** and accept the tackle-vs.-guard ambiguity? Or do you bet on **Freeling's** near-perfect testing profile and accept that he has fewer college starts than some junior college players?

[**Daniel Jeremiah**](https://www.steelers.com/news/on-the-clock-ranking-the-top-ots-in-the-26-draft) has made it clear: there are seven OTs with first-round potential — and after that group, there's a significant gap. The run on tackles in Round 1 is coming. The only question is in what order.

**Is Freeling's 9.99 RAS enough to make him your OT1? Or does Mauigoa's track record of dominance — two sacks allowed in two seasons — win out over the combine darling?**
