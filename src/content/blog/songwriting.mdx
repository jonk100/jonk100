Music is mathematical. There are specific frequencies, intervals, and harmonic ratios that the human ear finds pleasing. But writing a song requires more than just assembling mathematically correct chords; it requires emotional intent.

## The Sonic Bloom Experiment
When I built *Sonic Bloom*, an interactive generative music sequencer, I wanted to explore this intersection. The app uses the WebAudio API to synthesize sounds in real-time based on mathematical patterns. 

However, pure randomness sounds chaotic. To make it sound like *music*, I had to constrain the algorithms within musical scales and time signatures. 

## The Human Element
No matter how complex the algorithm, the best songs always stem from human emotion. Writing a song is about capturing a specific feeling in a bottle. The chords provide the setting, the melody provides the characters, and the rhythm provides the pacing. 

Whether I'm writing a folk song on an acoustic guitar or programming a synthesizer in JavaScript, the goal remains the same: to make the listener *feel* something.
