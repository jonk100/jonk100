Screenwriting is often romanticized as a purely creative endeavor—waiting for inspiration to strike in a coffee shop. But in reality, writing a screenplay is closer to software architecture than poetry. 

## The Blueprint
Just as you wouldn't start coding a complex application without a system design, you shouldn't write a script without a structural outline. For my project, *Her Majesty's Displeasure*, I spent weeks building a "Series Bible". 

This bible acts as the state container for the story. It holds:
* Character arcs (the state machines of the narrative)
* Worldbuilding rules (the global constants)
* Thematic goals (the business logic)

## Discipline Over Motivation
You don't always feel like coding, and you definitely don't always feel like writing. The key to finishing a 100-page script is setting a strict daily word count. Even if the words are bad, you have to push them to the page. You can always refactor a bad draft; you can't refactor a blank page.
